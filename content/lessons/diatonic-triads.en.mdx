Build a triad on every degree of the major scale, using only notes of the scale, and
you get seven chords. Their order is the same in **every** major key:

| Degree | I | ii | iii | IV | V | vi | vii° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Type | major | minor | minor | major | major | minor | diminished |
| in C | C | Dm | Em | F | G | Am | B° |
| in E♭ | E♭ | Fm | Gm | A♭ | B♭ | Cm | D° |

Roman numerals are the language musicians use to talk about harmony: "vi" is the
minor chord on the 6th degree in any key. Upper case means major here, lower case
minor, and ° diminished. Woodshed always writes the chord type next to the numeral,
though, so you don't have to memorise the upper/lower case.

Add the next third of the scale to vii° and in C you get B – D – F – A: **Bm7♭5**,
the half-diminished seventh chord. More on that in unit 2.

## The diatonic triads

Play all seven chords in a row, I to vii°. Once that feels easy in a key, you know its
entire harmonic material. Bonus: you get a number for your **voice leading** – how many
semitones your voices moved in total. Lower is better, and Woodshed remembers your
best. The number only appears when every chord has the same number of notes.
